About

Chess, explained the way a person would.

Chronael is a free chess coach for people who are just starting out. It plays with you, and after every move it tells you, in ordinary words, what that move actually did.

The trouble with chess engines

Engines are brilliant at chess and hopeless at teaching it. Ask one about a beginner’s move and this is what you get:

A chess engine 3. Nxe5?? −2.9
best: 3. Bc4 +0.4
Chronael

“Careful: the other knight can take yours for free. Bring out your bishop first and everything stays safe.”

Both say the same thing. Only one of them helps someone who started playing last week.

What happens after you move

  1. Stockfish checks it

    Stockfish, one of the strongest chess engines there is, runs inside your browser. It scores the position before and after your move and finds the best alternative. Lose about one and a half pawns’ worth and it counts as a mistake; three or more is a blunder.

  2. The facts are put into plain words

    Chess software writes a move as Nxe5. Chronael turns that into “the knight captures the pawn”, and does the same for the better move and for whatever your opponent is now threatening.

  3. A language model says it kindly

    Only those plain facts go to a small AI model, which turns them into a sentence or two. It never sees the board and never judges the chess, so it can’t make up a move. If it’s unavailable, Chronael writes the sentence itself.

The Magnus bot

One opponent is a neural network trained on Magnus Carlsen’s games. Given a position, it predicts the move he would play. In testing, its first choice matched his real move 27% of the time. A typical position has around thirty legal moves, so that is far better than guessing, but it is an imitation of his style, not the man himself. It runs on your device after a one-time 24 MB download.

What’s inside

Lessons
How each piece moves, then 15 short drills on checkmates, winning material, the opening and the endgame. Every accepted answer has been checked by Stockfish.
Puzzles
A daily puzzle, plus 3,058 rated puzzles from the Lichess public database, from 528 to 2500. Your puzzle rating moves as you solve them.
Your mistakes
Mistakes you make against the bot are saved and come back as puzzles: straight away, then after 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60 days while you keep getting them right.
Game review
After each game: an accuracy score, every move colour-coded, and the three moments that decided it, with the better move for you to find.
Playing friends
Send a link. A small server keeps both boards in sync and rejects illegal moves. If someone leaves, the other player can claim the game after a minute.

How it’s run

  • Free. No account, no adverts.
  • Private. Your progress is stored in your own browser. The only things that leave your device are online games and the plain-English facts sent to the coach.
  • Open source. The code is on GitHub.
  • Works anywhere. Once loaded it works offline, and it installs as an Android app.
